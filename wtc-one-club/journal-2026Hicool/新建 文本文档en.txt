# Why I Kept Returning to Manycore Technology

**01 Global Business Opportunities · 2025–2026**

Over the past two years, I have visited Manycore Technology several times.

Not because every visit was expected to lead to a project.

Not because there was always a specific transaction to discuss.

Sometimes, we simply wanted to understand each other better.

And that is precisely why I think the story is worth recording.

## The First Conversation Is Usually About Understanding

Manycore Technology is one of the technology companies that has attracted considerable attention in Hangzhou.

But what interested me was not simply its technology or its growth story.

It was a more practical question:

**How does a Chinese technology company build a genuinely international business?**

My first visit to Manycore Technology was an exchange focused on international expansion.

We talked about overseas markets, local customers, product experience, international teams and the practical challenges of entering Southeast Asia.

The conversation gradually moved beyond the usual discussion of whether a market has potential.

It became more about what happens after a company decides to enter that market.

Who are the local customers?

How do they think differently?

What needs to change in the product?

How should a company build local capabilities?

These questions may sound simple.

In practice, they are often where international expansion becomes difficult.

That was one of the things that stayed with me after the first visit.

**Going global is not simply about taking a product from one country to another. It is about learning how to operate in a different environment.**

## Then We Went Back Again

One visit is easy.

Returning several times is different.

Over the past two years, we have organized and participated in several exchanges, study visits and industry discussions involving Manycore Technology.

Each conversation had a slightly different context.

Sometimes we focused on the company itself.

Sometimes we discussed Southeast Asia.

Sometimes we talked about how Chinese technology companies can build local capabilities and better understand customers in overseas markets.

We also returned to Manycore Technology's global headquarters in Hangzhou.

The more often we met, the more I appreciated something that is easy to overlook in international business:

**A relationship does not have to begin with a transaction.**

It can begin with curiosity.

Then come more conversations.

Then understanding.

Then trust.

And only after that does it become clear what, if anything, the two sides might eventually do together.

## “Going Out” Is Not the Same as “Going In”

One idea came up repeatedly in our conversations about Chinese companies expanding into Southeast Asia:

**Going out is relatively easy to describe. Going in is much harder.**

A company can establish an overseas entity.

It can hire local employees.

It can begin selling to customers abroad.

But genuine localization requires something deeper.

It means understanding how local customers make decisions.

Understanding the business environment.

Understanding local culture.

Understanding the people you need to work with.

And sometimes, even reconsidering how a product should be presented and experienced in a particular market.

That is why I increasingly think of internationalization not simply as geographic expansion.

**In many ways, it is the process of learning how to build a business again in a different environment.**

Our repeated exchanges with Manycore Technology have given me an opportunity to observe that process through practical conversations rather than a single formal visit.

## Why Keep Going Back?

There is a simple reason.

Because good relationships become more valuable with time.

The first conversation gives you an introduction.

The second gives you context.

The third often gives you trust.

And after enough conversations, you no longer need to spend the first half of every meeting explaining who you are.

You can start with the real question.

For an international business network, I think this matters enormously.

The value of WTC Singapore is not simply that we can introduce a company to Singapore.

The greater value is what can happen after that introduction.

Who should the company meet?

Which local market might make sense?

Who has experience there?

Who can provide a useful perspective?

And if the first conversation does not immediately lead anywhere, who will still be there six months or a year later?

**That continuity is part of the value of a trusted network.**

## From a Company Visit to a Longer-Term Connection

Looking back, I think the Manycore Technology experience fits naturally into the WTC One Club journal.

Because WTC One Club was never intended to be simply a place where people attend an event and exchange a few business cards.

It is built around something more fundamental:

**bringing the right people into the same network and giving relationships enough time to develop.**

Over the past two years, we have returned to Manycore Technology several times.

We have seen the company in different settings, continued conversations with its team, and explored different questions around international business.

Some conversations may eventually become opportunities.

Some may simply remain valuable conversations.

Both are worthwhile.

Because international business is rarely built through one perfect meeting.

It is built through repeated contact, shared understanding and trust accumulated over time.

And sometimes, the most meaningful signal is simply this:

**you keep finding reasons to meet again.**

That is what I value about our relationship with Manycore Technology.

And that is also what I believe WTC One Club should create more of:

**not one meeting, not one introduction, and not one transaction — but a network in which the right relationships have the time and space to become meaningful.**
