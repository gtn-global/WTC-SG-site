The New-Energy Investment Banker Who Flew in from Hong Kong Pinpointed What Outbound Ventures Overlook Most

02 Capital Gravity Field 2025

At this salon, the original plan was to discuss the rather broad topic of "how capital markets view Chinese new-energy companies going global." What struck me most was a remark from someone in the room — a practitioner who has long worked in international investment banking, focused on the new-energy space and covering Asia-Pacific markets. He attended in a personal capacity and was not speaking for any institution, so I will not name the institution here; I only reconstruct the judgment and the conversation itself.

His point, roughly: international capital does not look at Chinese new-energy companies and ask whether their technology leads the world — most international investors already take that as given. What actually decides whether a company wins international capital's favor is whether "its governance structure is something overseas investors can read and trust." Technological leadership answers "is it worth investing in"; governance answers "do we dare to invest" — two different questions that many companies have folded into one.

"We discussed many cases of Chinese new-energy companies going global, and it eventually struck me that the default question everyone asks is 'how do we make foreigners believe in our technology,' whereas what this banker friend said that day was 'how do we make foreigners believe in our corporate governance' — the gap between those two may be larger than imagined, and easier to overlook."

— Bruce Dong, Secretary-General & COO, WTC Singapore

The conversation produced no concrete investment intent or partnership, but it changed the questions I later ask companies in similar settings — instead of "what is your technological edge," I now tend to ask first: "how much of your board can overseas investors actually read?"

Notes by Bruce Dong
Secretary-General & COO, WTC Singapore
