CIFTIS 2026 Concludes: WTC Singapore Connects Capital, Innovation and Global Markets, Charting a New Path for Chinese Companies Going Global

01 Global Business Opportunities September 2026 · Beijing

From 9 to 13 September, the 2026 China International Fair for Trade in Services (CIFTIS) was held at Beijing's Shougang Park under the theme “Global Services, Shared Prosperity.” As the officially licensed member of the World Trade Centers Association (WTCA) in Singapore, WTC Singapore took part in depth under the banner “Connecting Capital, Innovation and Global Markets.” Across just two days — 10 and 11 September — one flagship event, two roundtable dialogues and one research report were delivered in succession. From a “first stop for going global” to a “global ecosystem,” WTC Singapore brought its CIFTIS programme to a close with four landmark events.

01 Two Days, Four Events — WTC Singapore's CIFTIS Agenda

This year's CIFTIS drew more than 1,830 exhibitors and, for the first time, introduced a dedicated “Going Global Zone.” As a key node in the WTCA global network, WTC Singapore kept a full and tightly packed schedule:

10 September | The “Rooted in Free Trade, Connected to the World” flagship event at the WTCA main pavilion, where global free trade zone service partners made a collective debut and two major international cooperation outcomes were released.

11 September | The roundtable “Southeast Asia as a Hub — AI and Going Global as Growth Engines.”

11 September | The roundtable “Bridging Through Diaspora Networks — Globalisation Pathways and Ecosystem Building for the Life and Health Industry.”

11 September | The global launch of the China New Energy Power & Electrical Going-Global Research Report.

Beyond these, on 10 September WTC Singapore Chairman Blake Zhang attended the International Symposium on the Multilateral Trading System and Global Trade Network Development; on 11 September he attended the 2026 China Enterprises Going Global Forum and the Going-Global Integrated Service Ecosystem Partners Conference. Zhu Xiaolan, Chair of the WTCA Asia Pacific Trade and Commerce Committee and Chief Representative & CEO of WTC Singapore, joined the China Enterprises International Compliance Forum to exchange views on compliance in international operations.

02 10 September | Rooted in Free Trade, Connected to the World: Global FTZ Service Partners Make Their Debut

On the afternoon of 10 September, the “Rooted in Free Trade, Connected to the World” flagship event took place at the WTCA main pavilion. Led by the WTCA Asia Pacific Trade and Commerce Committee, it brought together the World Trade Centers of Singapore and Chongqing alongside leading service institutions from the Hainan and Shanghai free trade zones. In her address, Zhu Xiaolan, Chief Representative & CEO of WTC Singapore, extended an invitation to the business community present: “As Chinese companies map out their global footprint, WTC Singapore stands ready to be their first stop abroad.”

She noted that the WTCA network spans more than 95 countries and regions and over 320 cities, connecting more than 750,000 member businesses, making it one of the most influential trade and business platforms in the world. Building on Singapore's position as an ASEAN financial and trading hub, WTC Singapore works through three core businesses — WTC ONE Club, the international business club; GlobalX, the go-global accelerator; and WTC Fund, the innovation capital ecosystem — to give companies a one-stop service covering market entry, compliance and localisation, opening a two-way corridor between ASEAN and China.

At the event, WTC Singapore and WTC Chongqing held a joint flag ceremony, formally establishing a strategic partnership. With Singapore as an ASEAN financial and trading hub and Chongqing as a core node on the New International Land-Sea Trade Corridor, their complementary strengths will build a more efficient cross-border service bridge for companies in both locations. Free trade zone service partners including FTP Free Trade Stop No.1, the Yangtze River Delta Innovation Alliance and Deya Fangzhou (Boao Lecheng) also shared their practices, sketching a cooperation map stretching from Hainan to Shanghai and from Boao Lecheng to western China's land-sea corridor.

Two major outcomes were also released that day. First, the Global Free Trade Zone Health Industry Linkage Mechanism was officially launched. Initiated by WTCA together with Hainan Boao Lecheng, the Shanghai free trade zone and health service institutions from multiple free trade zones worldwide, it promotes medical technology exchange, health tourism referrals, industrial resource sharing and mutual recognition of standards. Second, the WTCA “Century Brand Global Flagship Store” was officially established in Sanya, Hainan. Leveraging the Hainan Free Trade Port's offshore duty-free policy and its positioning as an international tourism and consumption centre, it creates a new showcase and trading platform for international century-old brands entering the Chinese market.

03 11 September | Two Roundtables on AI and Health: How Chinese Companies Truly “Go In”

On 11 September, Bruce Dong, Secretary-General and COO of WTC Singapore, moderated the roundtable “Southeast Asia as a Hub — AI and Going Global as Growth Engines” at the WTCA pavilion. Four speakers drawn from technology, finance, industry and overseas operations formed a “technology — finance — industry — overseas” panel.

Dong observed: “AI will no longer be just an efficiency tool for companies going global. It will reshape the underlying logic of how they choose markets, allocate capital and build local teams. Whoever applies AI at the decision-making level first — not merely at the execution level — will move faster through the Southeast Asia hub.”

Cai Weizhao, Director of Overseas Markets and Ecosystem at Yonyou, pointed out that the real common bottleneck for Chinese companies going global is not model capability, but the fact that domestic and overseas data and business systems are not connected. Only by turning “system silos” into a network can AI extend from execution-level efficiency into decision-making. Li Ning, General Manager of the International Business Department at Bank of Ningbo's Beijing Branch, presented the bank's “Global Win” service system, which has served more than 1,000 companies going global. Huang Shasha, Vice President of Guolian Shares, explained how industrial internet platforms convert the cluster advantages of China's industrial belts into systematic, collective strength for going global. Drawing on the history of Japanese companies expanding abroad, Yuma Nashiro, President of NSR Inc., cautioned: “AI may lower the organisational cost of entering a market, but it will not eliminate the time needed to build local trust.” Dong concluded that in the AI era, what ultimately determines success in going global is the speed at which a company combines technology with capital, data, industry, compliance and local trust systems.

At 14:30, the roundtable “Bridging Through Diaspora Networks — Globalisation Pathways and Ecosystem Building for the Life and Health Industry” followed, with five speakers from the diaspora network, overseas service networks, industry practice and the innovation frontline forming a four-dimensional “diaspora — industry — overseas — platform” panel.

In his opening remarks, Dong offered a core judgement: “In healthcare going global, the product is only the entry ticket; the real challenge is entering the market.” To truly enter a foreign country's healthcare system, a company must clear three gates: regulatory access, market trust and long-term capital. “Diaspora networks provide trust, the World Trade Centers network provides the channel, and capital markets provide patience — none of the three can be missing.”

Xu Xiaojie, Standing Committee Member of the Beijing Federation of Returned Overseas Chinese and Chairman of the Daxing District Federation of Returned Overseas Chinese, noted that the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei Life and Health Industry International Cooperation Alliance has linked more than 80 overseas service stations and brought together over a hundred overseas Chinese entrepreneurs and professionals. Xie Yi, Director of the Indonesia Comprehensive Service Station at the China North (TEDA) One-Stop Going-Global Service Base, and Sun Xiaogang, Secretary-General of the Association of French Incubators (AIFC), shared front-line observations on the Southeast Asian and European markets respectively. Rui Zhifu, founder of Lantian Kangtai, and Guo Liang, Executive Director of Zhongke Lijian, confirmed this from industry practice: product competitiveness is only the starting point of internationalisation — the real passport is local registration and trust-building.

04 11 September | Global Launch of the China New Energy Power & Electrical Going-Global Research Report

That day, the global launch ceremony of the China New Energy Power & Electrical Going-Global Research Report was held at the WTCA pavilion. Jointly released by WTC Singapore and Hejun Consulting's Power & Electrical Division under the theme “From Product Export to Global Ecosystem,” the report presents a systematic study of the pathways, market landscape and trends in the globalisation of China's new energy power and electrical industry.

The report notes that getting products into overseas markets is only the starting point of international operations. Moving from “product export” to “capability export” and then to “ecosystem export,” companies must progressively complete the transition from an export business to global operations, as the core of international competition shifts from single-product capability to comprehensive operating capability. This joint release combines industry research with an international network: on one hand it maps the practice and trends of Chinese new energy power and electrical companies going global from an industry perspective; on the other, it uses the WTCA global network to strengthen the flow of information and resources between Chinese companies and overseas markets and industry partners, providing decision-making reference as companies calibrate their international strategy and open new markets.

05 The Close Is Not the End — After the “First Stop,” Connecting Globally Has Only Just Begun

From the collective debut of global free trade zone partners on 10 September to the two roundtables and the report launch on 11 September, WTC Singapore moved at a high-frequency, high-intensity pace, completing a full arc at this year's CIFTIS from “brand presence” to “delivered outcomes.” Behind the four events lies a single logic: with the WTCA global network as the foundation and capital, trust and channels as the pillars, WTC Singapore opens broader space for cooperation as Chinese companies “bring in” and “go out.”

The “first stop” for going global is now in place; the road to connecting globally has only just begun. On the next leg of the journey, WTC Singapore will be at your side.

06 About WTC Singapore

About the World Trade Centers Association (WTCA): Founded in 1970, the World Trade Centers Association is a non-profit, non-political global trade organisation headquartered in New York. Its network covers nearly 100 countries and regions and more than 300 World Trade Centers, dedicated to promoting international trade and investment cooperation and providing network support for companies to build cross-border business connections and expand into international markets.

About WTC Singapore: WTC Singapore is the officially licensed member of the World Trade Centers Association (WTCA) in Singapore. Operating through three business pillars — WTC ONE Club, GlobalX and WTC Fund — it is dedicated to connecting global capital, innovation resources and market opportunities, and to providing enterprises with cross-border resource matching and support for global landing.
