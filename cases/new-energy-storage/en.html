From China to a Southeast Asia Power Operations Hub

A new energy company specializing in commercial and industrial energy storage and solar inverters wanted to use Singapore as a hub to manage product certification, grid access, and after-sales operations across multiple Southeast Asian markets. Here is the full story of that journey.

New Energy · Storage China → Singapore GlobalX Accelerator Regional Operations Hub

Setup time 4 months (originally estimated at 10 months) · 9 local partners onboarded · 4 Southeast Asian power markets entered

Background and Challenge

The company specializes in commercial and industrial energy storage systems and solar inverters, and had already entered parts of the Southeast Asian market through export trade. But orders relied entirely on local agents, leaving the company without direct control over grid-access standards, local certification timelines, or on-site service — resulting in long delivery cycles and slow after-sales response.

Leadership determined that as distributed energy and storage policies rolled out across Southeast Asia, the company needed to build an operations hub within the region — one with international credibility, mature financial and legal systems, and the ability to navigate grid standards across the Philippines, Indonesia and Vietnam simultaneously. Singapore was the first choice, but the team lacked connections to local testing and certification bodies, power engineering contractors, and bonded warehousing. Internal estimates put independent setup at at least 10 months.

"Every country's grid access standard is different. Just figuring out who to talk to for certification and who could deliver on-site engineering work took us the better part of a year on our own."

— Bruce Dong
